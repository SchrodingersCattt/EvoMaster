Title: Phonons: Theory
URL: https://vasp.at/wiki/Phonons:_Theory
============================================================
Phonons are the collective excitation of nuclei in an extended periodic system.
Taylor expansion in ionic displacements
To compute the phonon modes and frequencies we start by Taylor expanding the total energy ( E
) around the set of equilibrium positions of the nuclei (
\{{R}^0\}
)
E(\{{R}\})=
E(\{{R}^0\})+
Σ_{Iα} { E(\{{R^0}\})}{ R_{Iα}} (R_{Iα}-R^0_{Iα})+
Σ_{Iα Jβ} { E(\{{R}^0\})}{ R_{Iα} R_{Jβ}}
(R_{Iα}-R^0_{Iα}) (R_{Jβ}-R^0_{Jβ})+
O({R}^3),
where
\{{R}\}
the positions of the nuclei.
The first derivative of the total energy with respect to the nuclei corresponds to the forces
F_{Iα} (\{{R}^0\}) =
- . { E(\{{R}\})}{ R_{Iα}} |_{{R} ={R^0}}
,
and the second derivative to the second-order force-constants
Φ_{Iα Jβ} (\{{R}^0\}) =
. { E(\{{R}\})}{ R_{Iα} R_{Jβ}} |_{{R} ={R^0}}
=
- . { F_{Iα}(\{{R}\})}{ R_{Jβ}} |_{{R} ={R^0}}.
Changing variables in the Taylor expansion of the total energy with
u_{Iα} = R_{Iα}-R^0_{Iα}
that corresponds to the displacement of the atoms with respect to their equilibrium position
R^0_{Iα}
leads to
E(\{{R}\})=
E(\{{R}^0\})+
Σ_{Iα} -F_{Iα} (\{{R}^0\}) u_{Iα}+
Σ_{Iα Jβ} Φ_{Iα Jβ} (\{{R}^0\}) u_{Iα} u_{Jβ} +
O({R}^3)
Dynamical matrix and phonon modes
If we take the system to be in equilibrium, the forces on the atoms are zero and then the Hamiltonian of the system is
H =
{1}{2} Σ_{Iα} M_I u^2_{Iα} +
{1}{2} Σ_{Iα Jβ} Φ_{Iα Jβ} u_{Iα} u_{Jβ},
with
M_I the mass of the I
-th nucleus.
The equation of motion is then given by
M_I u_{Iα} = -
Φ_{Iα Jβ} u_{Jβ}.
We then look for solutions in the form of plane waves traveling parallel to the wave vector
{q}
, i.e.
{u}_{Iα,ν}({q},t) = {1}{2} {1}{M_I}
\{
A^ν({q}) _{Iα,ν}({q})
e^{ i [{q} · {{R}}_I -ω_ν({q})t]}+
A^{ν*}({q}) ^*_{Iα,ν}({q})
e^{-i [{q} · {{R}}_I -ω_ν({q})t]}
\}
where
_{Iα,ν}({q})
are the phonon mode eigenvectors and
A^ν({q})
the amplitudes.
Replacing it in the equation of motion we obtain the following eigenvalue problem
Σ_{Jβ} D_{Iα Jβ} ({q})
_{Jβ,ν}({q}) =
ω_ν({q})^2 _{Iα,ν}({q})
with
D_{Iα Jβ} ({q}) =
{1}{M_I M_J} Φ_{Iα Jβ} e^{i{q} · ({R}_J-{R}_I)}
the dynamical matrix in the harmonic approximation.
Now by solving the eigenvalue problem above we can obtain the phonon modes
_{Iα,ν}({q})
and frequencies
ω_ν({q})
at any arbitrary q
point.
We can write the positions of the atoms in the supercell
{R}_I
in terms of integer multiples of the lattice vectors of the unit cell
{l}
such that
{R}_I arrow {R}_{li} = {l} + {r}_i
with
{r}_i
being the position of the ion in the unit cell.
The force constants then become
Φ_{Iα, Jβ} arrow Φ_{liα, l'jβ}
.
The dynamical matrix is then given by
D_{iα jβ} ({q}) =
{1}{M_i M_j} Σ_{l} Φ_{liα, 0jβ} e^{-i{q} · ({l}+{r}_i-{r}_j)},
with
{l}
chosen using the minimal image convention.
This allows us to compute the phonons in the unit cell using the following equation
Σ_{jβ} D_{iα jβ} ({q})
_{jβ,ν}({q}) =
ω_ν({q})^2 _{iα,ν}({q})
with the dynamical matrix having dimension
3n
with n
the number of atoms in the unit cell.
Long-range interatomic force constants (LO-TO splitting)
For semiconductors or insulators, the electronic screening of the ions is incomplete which leads to long-range (LR) interatomic force constants. To compute them explicitly would require infinitely large supercell calculations. For practical calculations, a finite size truncation is needed which leads to Gibbs oscillations in the phonon dispersion. Fortunately, this long-range behavior can be modeled by looking at the analytic form of the ion-ion contribution to the total energy.
For that, follow the approach outlined in Ref.

and start by splitting the second-order force constants into short-range and long-range parts,
Φ_{Iα Jβ} = Φ_{Iα Jβ}^{SR} + Φ_{Iα Jβ}^{LR}
with the long-range part being obtained from the analytic derivative of the long-range part of the ion-ion contribution to the total energy
E_{ion-ion}
. This contribution is typically evaluated using an Ewald sum technique in which we separate the ion-ion contribution to the total energy into two part, one is evaluated in real space and captures the short-range part and the other one in reciprocal space which captures the long-range part
E_{ion-ion} = E^{SR}_{ion-ion} + E^{LR}_{ion-ion}
. The separation is governed by an Ewald parameter

which represents a truncation length.
This leads to the following analytical expression for the long-range interatomic force constants,
Φ_{Iα Jβ}^{LR} =
{4π e^2}{_0}
Σ_{G}
{({G} · {Z}^*_{Iα})({G} · {Z}^*_{Jβ})}
{{G} · ε^∞ · {G}}
e^{i{G} · ({R}_J-{R}_I)}
e^{-{G} · ε^∞ · {G}}{4^2}
with
ε^∞
the ion-clamped dielectric tensor,
{Z}^*_{Iα}
the Born effective charges, α
the Ewald parameter which is chosen such that the contributions from
e^{-{G} · ε^∞ · {G}}{4^2}
are negligible within a certain
{G}
vector cutoff sphere
PHON_G_CUTOFF
.
This also allows us to separate the dynamical matrix into short and long-range parts
D_{Iα Jβ} ({q}) = D^{SR}_{Iα Jβ} ({q}) + D^{LR}_{Iα Jβ} ({q}),
with the long-range part of the dynamical matrix
D^{LR}_{iα jβ} ({q}) =
{4π e^2}{_0}
Σ_{G}
Σ_{l}
{[ ({G}+{q}) · {Z}^*_{iα} ]
[ ({G}+{q}) · {Z}^*_{jβ} ]}
{({G}+{q}) · ε^∞ · ({G}+{q})}
e^{i({q}+{G}) · ({l}+{r}_i-{r}_j)}
e^{-({G}+{q}) · ε^∞ · ({G}+{q})}{4^2}.
The equations above give us the practical method for computing the phonon dynamical matrices including the long-range force constants using a moderately sized supercell calculation with the steps:
Compute
Φ_{Iα Jβ}
using a finite size supercell
Compute
Φ_{Iα Jβ}^{SR}=Φ_{Iα Jβ}-Φ^{LR}_{Iα Jβ}
Compute
D^{SR}_{iα jβ}({q})
using
Φ_{Iα Jβ}^{SR}
Compute
D^{LR}_{iα jβ}({q})
in the unit cell and add to
D^{SR}_{iα jβ}({q})
The treatment is done automatically inside VASP using the
LPHON_POLAR =.TRUE. tag and specifying the dielectric tensor with PHON_DIELECTRIC and the Born effective charges with PHON_BORN_CHARGES .
