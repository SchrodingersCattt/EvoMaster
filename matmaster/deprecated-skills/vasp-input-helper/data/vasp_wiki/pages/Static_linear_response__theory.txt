============================================================
Let’s consider three types of static perturbations
atomic displacements
u_m
with
m=Iα

I=\{1..N_{atoms}\}

α=\{1..3\}

η_j

E_α

α=\{1..3\}
By performing a Taylor expansion of the total energy E
in terms of these perturbations we obtain

aligned
E(u,E,η) =
&E_0 + \\

&{^2 E}{ u_m E_α}

{^2 E}{ u_m η_j}

E_α η_j +
{terms of higher order}
aligned
The derivatives of the energy with respect to an electric field are the polarization, with respect to atomic displacements are the forces, with respect to changes in the lattice vectors are the stress tensor.
P_α = -{ E}{ E_α}
{polarization}
F_m = -_0{ E}{ u_m}
{forces}
σ_j = { E}{ η_j}
{stresses}
This leads to the following ‘clamped-ion’ or ‘frozen-ion’ definitions:
χ_{αβ} =
- {^2 E}{ E_α E_β} |_{u,η}
{dielectric susceptibility}
C_{jk} =
{^2 E}{ η_j η_k} |_{u,E}
{elastic tensor}
Φ_{mn}=_0 {^2 E}{ u_m u_n} |_{E,η}
{force-constants}
e_{α k} =
{^2 E}{ E_α η_k} |_{u}
{piezoelectric tensor}
Z^*_{mα}=-_0 {^2 E}{ u_m E_α} |_{η}
{Born effective charges}
_{mj}=-_0 {^2 E}{ u_m η_j} |_{E}
{force response internal strain tensor}
To compare with experimental results, however, the static response properties should take into account the ionic relaxation. This follows from the Taylor expansion above by looking at the ionic positions where the energy is minimal:
E(E,η) = {min}_u E(u,E,η)
The physical ‘relaxed-ion’ tensors are
aligned
χ_{αβ} &= χ_{αβ} +
_0^{-1} Z^*_{mα} (Φ)^{-1}_{mn} Z^*_{nβ}
{dielectric susceptibility}\\
C_{jk} &= C_{jk} +
_0^{-1} _{mj} (Φ)^{-1}_{mn} _{nk}
{elastic tensor}\\
e_{α j} &= e_{α j} +
_0^{-1}Z^*_{mα} (Φ)^{-1}_{mn} _{nj}
{piezoelectric tensor}
aligned
